## 8. STP Framework Analysis

### 8.1 Segmentation

Nava Creations' market can be segmented across three primary dimensions:

**By Occasion / Need:**
| Segment | Need |
|---------|------|
| Bridal | Premium, often custom, once-in-a-lifetime purchase |
| Festival | Trend-led, seasonal, colour-coordinated |
| Everyday | Regular, affordable, varied |
| Gift | Convenience, packaging, occasion-fit |
| Bulk/Return Gift | Volume, pricing, logistics reliability |
| Customisation | Unique design, brand story, exclusivity |

**By Price Sensitivity:**
- Low sensitivity: Bridal, Customisation
- Medium sensitivity: Festival, Gift, Everyday
- High sensitivity: Bulk/Return Gift (per-unit price matters)

**By Purchase Journey:**
- Impulse: Everyday, Gift
- Considered: Festival, Bridal
- Complex/Relationship: Bulk, Customisation

### 8.2 Targeting

> [!NOTE]
> The following targeting recommendations are **hypothetical** and should be refined based on actual sales data and customer interviews.

**Primary Target (MVP focus):** Segments A (Everyday) + C (Festival) drive volume. Segment B (Bridal) + E (Bulk) drive revenue. These four should receive priority attention in product presentation and feature design.

**Secondary Target (Post-MVP):** Segment D (Gift) and F (Customisation) are valuable but require more complex workflows; scaffold in Phase 1.

**Targeting rationale:**
- Everyday + Festival buyers are reachable via Instagram and Google SEO at relatively low cost
- Bridal + Bulk orders have high AOV, justifying higher customer acquisition investment
- Both segments require structured enquiry workflows not available via Instagram DMs alone — this is a primary website value proposition

### 8.3 Positioning

Unlike mass-produced options on large marketplaces, Nava Creations brings personal attention, customisation, and the warmth of a small brand that knows its craft.*

**Positioning Pillars:**
| Pillar | Claim | Proof Point Needed |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Handcrafted / Artisan | Products made with care, not mass-produced | ❓ Artisan/maker story; workshop photos |
| Occasion-perfect | Right product for every Indian moment | ✅ Broad product range confirmed |
| Personalised service | Custom orders, WhatsApp attention | ✅ Customisation confirmed |

| Accessible luxury | Premium feel at accessible price | ❓ Pricing to be confirmed |

## 9. Value Proposition Canvas — Bridal Segment

> [!NOTE]
> This canvas is **hypothetical** and must be validated via bridal customer interviews before being used to guide design decisions.

### Customer Profile (Bridal Buyer)

**Jobs to Be Done:**
- Find beautiful, well-made bangles that complement her bridal outfit
- Get the right size and customisation without risk
- Manage the purchase without adding stress to wedding planning
- Feel confident the order will arrive on time

**Pains:**
| Pain | Intensity |
|------|-----------|
| Risk of wrong size with no easy exchange close to the wedding | 🔴 Critical |
| Delivery delay ruining the wedding-day look | 🔴 Critical |
| Product looking different in person vs photos | 🔴 High |
| Having to chase the seller for order status | 🟡 Medium |
| Difficulty communicating specific customisation requirements | 🟡 Medium |
| Feeling uncertain about seller reliability / legitimacy | 🟡 Medium |

**Gains:**
| Gain | Importance |
|------|------------|
| Confident she has the perfect bangle for her wedding day | ⭐ Critical |
| Stress-free purchase process | ⭐ High |
| Receiving the product well before the event | ⭐ High |
| Product that photographs beautifully (wedding photos) | ⭐ High |
| Feeling personally attended to | ⭐ Medium |
| Being able to show / share the product with family for approval | ⭐ Medium |

### Value Map (Nava Creations)

**Pain Relievers:**
- Detailed size guide + how-to-measure on website (relieves wrong-size pain)
- WhatsApp enquiry flow with direct communication (relieves trust/communication pain)
- Explicit lead time and "order by X date to receive by Y" messaging (relieves delivery-delay pain)
- Multiple high-quality photos showing real colour in natural light (relieves colour-accuracy pain)
- Clear customisation enquiry form with process overview (relieves spec-communication pain)

**Gain Creators:**
- Curated "Bridal Collection" with Kundan-work highlights
- Shareable wishlist link (bride can share with family for approval)
- WhatsApp order confirmation and dispatch notifications (peace of mind)
- Real bridal customer photos (social proof + inspiration) ❓ to be collected
- Option for a matching hair accessory set ("complete the bridal look")

### 11.1 Overview

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Testing phase | Pre-launch (prototype or staging site) |
| Number of participants | 5 (one per primary segment) |
| Testing format | Moderated, remote (Google Meet + screen share) or in-person in Bengaluru |
| Session duration | 45–60 minutes per participant |
| Devices | Participant's own mobile phone (Android/iPhone) — mobile-first |
| Incentive | ❓ To be determined (e.g., Nava Creations gift voucher) |

### 11.2 Participant Recruitment Profile

| P# | Segment | Profile |
|----|---------|---------|
| P1 | A — Everyday Buyer | Woman 25–35, urban Bengaluru, buys accessories online regularly |
| P2 | B — Bridal Customer | Woman 24–32 who is engaged / recently married; bought or shopped for bridal accessories |
| P3 | C — Festival Shopper | Woman 30–45, buys new accessories for festivals; Diwali/Navratri buyer |
| P4 | E — Bulk Buyer | Person who organised a wedding or large event in the past 2 years |
| P5 | F — Customisation | Person who has previously requested a custom product from a small brand |

### 11.3 Test Tasks

| Task # | Task Description | Segment Focus | Success Criterion |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| T1 | "You've seen Nava Creations on Instagram. Find a bangle you like, check if it comes in your size, and add it to your cart." | A, C | Finds product, navigates to PDP, adds to cart without confusion; ≤ 3 mins |
| T2 | "You are looking for bangles for your wedding. Find the bridal collection and start a customisation enquiry." | B | Lands on bridal collection, finds enquiry CTA, completes form with no assistance; ≤ 5 mins |
| T3 | "Find a festival occasion collection and check whether the items can be delivered before Diwali [set date 10 days away]." | C | Navigates to festival collection, checks delivery estimate at checkout or product page; ≤ 4 mins |
| T4 | "You need 150 bangles as return gifts for a wedding. Find out how to place a bulk order and submit an enquiry." | E | Finds bulk order page, understands MOQ/process, submits enquiry form; ≤ 5 mins |
| T5 | "You placed an order a week ago. Find your order status and the expected delivery date." | All | Finds order tracking (login or guest track), reads status, without asking for help; ≤ 2 mins |

### 11.4 Success Metrics

| Metric | Method | Target |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Task completion rate | Observer records success/fail per task | ≥ 80 % of tasks completed without assistance |
| Time on task | Timed by moderator | Within targets above |
| Error rate | Count of wrong turns / dead ends per task | ≤ 2 errors per task per participant |
| System Usability Scale (SUS) score | Post-session questionnaire (10 questions) | ≥ 70 (Good) |
| Participant-reported ease | "How easy was that?" 1–5 Likert after each task | ≥ 4 average |
| Number of unassisted completions | Observer records if help was given | Aim for 0 assists on T1, T5 |
| Confusion moments | Noted by observer (hesitation > 10 s, back-navigation) | Analysed qualitatively |

- Revised designs (if any) for second round of testing
